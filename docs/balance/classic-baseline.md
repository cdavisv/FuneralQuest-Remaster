# Balance report: classic rules

## What it says about the original design

*Hand-written reading of the generated tables below (regenerating with `pnpm --filter @fq/sim run sim -- --days 30 --towns 200 --players 8 --rules classic --out docs/balance/classic-baseline` overwrites this file; keep this section). The bots are the six `@fq/bots` personalities at "hard" skill; the win table measures their strategies under Classic rules, so read it as "what a committed strategy earns", not as a verdict on a personality. Comparison runs (40 towns each, not saved): the hidden-state oracle, mixed difficulty, and a town without the two PvP personalities.*

1. **Psychology is the dominant investment, and nothing caps it.** Sale BP per customer turn climbs from 23 at psych 1 to 84 at psych 12 (3.7x), almost linearly, while a class costs only 4 + 2 x level gold tags. Psych is 61% of all tag spending. The skill that drives it is the estimate (from psych 3 on, near certain by psych 8), which prints the exact spending limit: the closeness bonuses (+25 within $100, +35 within $50) then make up a quarter of all sale BP for good sellers. The share of the limit a sale captures rises from 62% at psych 1 to 92% at psych 10 and above.
2. **Hearse + psychology wins every town.** All 200 winners were one of the two personalities that buy both (the Shark 44%, the Builder 32%, against a fair share of 12.5%). The Scholar, which buys only psychology, sells best per turn (67 sale BP/turn) and leads 194 of 200 towns on day 15, but is passed in the last ten days: a Crown Sovereign adds 10 turns a day (15 to 23.4 turns), and turns multiply the value of every psych level. Turns and psych compound, so the right order (psych early, then hearses) is the whole strategy; everything else is a dead choice by comparison.
3. **The +200 exact-match bonus is mostly luck.** It lands on 0.6% of sales (1.3% for the best reader; 0.8% even for the oracle that knows the limit), because soft-sell items are priced in $50 steps and the limit is any dollar amount: only one limit in fifty can be hit at all. It is 2.4% of all BP. The real reward for reading a customer is the within-$50/$100 bonus, which the text never explains.
4. **Hidden information matters less than it looks.** The oracle (reads limit, loved flag and resistance) earns only 3 to 10% more per day than the fair hard bot of the same personality. Easy bots (no limit tracking, poor mood reading) earn 30% less than normal ones; normal and hard are within noise, so the deeper inferences (mood deltas, learned prices) add little once the estimate exists.
5. **Snowballing is weak; compounding is strong.** The leader ends at only 1.21x the median, and the day-1, day-5 and day-10 leaders go on to win 14%, 20% and 8% of the time. The top-seller and top-three mail bonuses do not create a runaway leader. Instead the late game belongs to whoever set up the turns-times-psych engine, and the lead changes hands around day 20.
6. **PvP pays the attacker and barely hurts the victim, and grows without limit.** A win takes 2% of the victim's total BP, so fights get richer as totals grow; the Saboteur makes 470 BP a day from fights (half its income) while victims lose only 11 to 91 BP a day (the leader, the favourite target, the most). Attackers who pick fights they expect to win (strength and PE bought) win 94.5% of battles; defence is not a choice anyone makes, because the original has no way to train defence except PE and strength, which only fighters buy. Theft is a dead choice: 0.79 tags per theft.
7. **Dead or near-dead choices.** Security (padlock, safe, smoke detector: 1.3% of spending) protects against losses that are tiny; the Twins' arson (6 gold) burns 5% of plots and wood tags that a sensible player has already converted to gold; Lucky Sparrow and Cursed Tiger move only luck, and luck only matters for risks (risk takers gain 30 to 80 BP a day from risks, under 7% of income) and events. Taking a risk at luck 50 is close to break-even against declining (4 to 10 BP).
8. **Wait for Customer behaves as specified:** 80.4% customers, 8.0% events, 7.4% risks, 4.3% scripts, matching `docs/classic-rules.md` section 3.

For the Expanded ruleset this suggests: cap or slow psychology (or make high levels trade off against something), make the estimate a range rather than the exact limit, explain or rescale the closeness bonuses, give turns a cost so the hearse is a choice, scale PvP gains less than linearly with the victim's total and give defence a real investment, and make security, luck and arson matter enough to be decisions.
