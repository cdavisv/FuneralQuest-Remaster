## Long description (Google Play, App Store, up to 4000 characters)

Count: 2593

```text
Welcome to Deathville, where business is always dying to get in.

You run a funeral parlor. Each day you get a handful of turns. Spend them waiting for customers, talking relatives into the silver casket when they came in for the pine one, and making sure the parlor down the road has a much worse week than you do.

HOW IT WORKS
- Wait for a customer, then pick your approach: sympathy, guilt, hard sell or soft sell. Know when to stop. Push too far and they walk out; push too little and you bury them at a loss.
- Spend your casket tags around town: the bank, the store, the cafe, the hospital and a few places you should not discuss in front of the minister.
- Visit your rivals. Be neighborly. Or not.
- Score Bury Points. The parlor with the most at season's end gets its name in the Book of Deeds, and everyone else gets a very good view of it.

PLAY IT YOUR WAY
- Solo Career: a whole town of computer-controlled rivals runs on your own device. No internet, no account, no data leaves your phone. Pick how many rivals, how clever they are, and whether you want a 30-day career or an endless one. Your careers are saved on the device, and you can export them to a file.
- Online towns (not in the first mobile release, which is solo-only): create a free account (or try it as a guest, who can play and read but not chat or write), join a town and share it with other players. Read the paper, chat in the cafe, send mail and see who is on top of the rankings. Rated 18+.
- Daily Challenge (the first mobile release has the offline practice day only, no leaderboard): one fresh run a day, one score on the leaderboard.
- Classic and Expanded rulesets: Classic is the 2001 game, faithfully rebuilt. Expanded keeps the rules you know and adds more places, more customers, a living town and a few things the original left out.

A REMASTER, NOT A REPLACEMENT
Funeral Quest was made in 2001 by Robinson Technologies. This free, open-source remaster rebuilds it from the original source so it runs on modern devices, with the original artwork restored as sharp vectors, a new interface that fits a phone or a wide screen, and the rules ported line by line.

WHAT TO EXPECT
Dark humor, cartoon violence, crude jokes, mild language and a lot of puns about the deceased. Death is a business here, and nobody takes it personally except the competition. There is simulated gambling with in-game money only. Online play includes chat and mail between players; there is no real-money content, no ads and no in-app purchases.

Free and open source. Source code and credits are available in the project repository. This product includes software written by Seth A. Robinson (www.rtsoft.com). This remaster is an independent project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Robinson Technologies.
```
